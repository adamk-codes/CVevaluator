# Nour Aziz
@ nour.aziz@example.com | 03 118 774 | Zahle, Lebanon

I have been writing Java professionally for a little over three years, all of it at Mount Lebanon Insurance, where I joined as a graduate developer and am now one of two people who maintain the claims platform.

The platform is a Spring Boot application backed by PostgreSQL. I work across most of it. The piece I am most pleased with is the claims intake rewrite I did last year: the old flow was a single 900-line service method that did validation, persistence and notification in one transaction, and a timeout anywhere in it left claims half-created. I split it into three stages with explicit transaction boundaries and made the notification step retryable, which took the manual cleanup work our support team was doing from about ten claims a week to none. I also added the first integration tests the module ever had, using Testcontainers against a real PostgreSQL because the H2 tests we had were passing on queries that failed in production.

Day to day I write REST endpoints with Spring Web, map entities with Spring Data JPA, and spend more time than I expected reading query plans. I found and fixed an N+1 on the claims list endpoint that was issuing 200-odd queries per page load; adding a fetch join brought a 3.4 second page down to 240 milliseconds. I am comfortable with JUnit 5 and Mockito and I write tests without being asked.

What I have not done: we deploy to two virtual machines with a shell script, so I have no Kubernetes experience beyond a course I took. There is no message broker in our stack, so I have read about Kafka but never run it. Our service is not distributed in any meaningful sense, and our traffic is low enough that I have never had to tune the JVM. I am telling you this because I would rather be asked about it than found out in the interview.

I hold a BS in Computer Science from the Lebanese University, Faculty of Sciences, 2021. Arabic and English fluent, French conversational.

Java 17, Spring Boot, Spring Data JPA, Hibernate, PostgreSQL, JUnit 5, Mockito, Testcontainers, Maven, Git, Jenkins.
